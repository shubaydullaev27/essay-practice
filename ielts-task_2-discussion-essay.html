Discussion essay guide

Understand the task, plan both sides, follow the four-paragraph structure, and use the phrase bank while you write.

What is a discussion essay?

You are given two views on one topic. You discuss both views fairly. In most questions you must also give your own opinion.

Question type“Some people say … Others say … Discuss both views and give your opinion.”
You mustExplain why each side thinks the way they do, then state which side you support and why.
Key wordsBOTH views and YOUR opinion. Missing either one lowers your Task Response score.

Length: at least 250 words · about 40 minutes

Two common versions

“Discuss both views and give your opinion”: opinion is required, so give it in the introduction and the conclusion.
“Discuss both views”: you may stay neutral, but you still need a clear conclusion.

Idea generation in three steps

1. Analyse the question

Write the topic, the task, and your position before anything else.
Topic: the relevance of libraries · Task: discuss BOTH views and give YOUR opinion · Position: libraries are still important

2. Ask a question for each side

“Why do critics think …?” and “Why do others, including myself, believe …?”

3. Give each answer a main idea and an explanation

Choose the 2 strongest ideas for each side. One main idea, then one or two lines that explain it.
Main idea: atmosphere · Explanation: libraries are more than reading places (distraction-free, learning, cooperation)

The plan for each topic is shown above its model essay.

Four paragraphs

Introduction (3 sentences)

General statement · paraphrase both views · your opinion.

Body 1: the other side

Explain why critics think the way they do. Two main ideas, each with an explanation.

Body 2: your side

Start by accepting the other side is reasonable, then say which side you support. Two main ideas, each with an explanation.

Conclusion (1–2 sentences)

Restate your opinion and the main points. No new ideas.

Why Body 1 is the other side: ending on your own view leaves the reader with your opinion.

Phrase bank

Introduction

  • … have always played a crucial role in …
  • However, many people question …
  • … while others oppose this view.
  • In my opinion, … / I firmly believe that …

Presenting the other side

  • Critics of … point out numerous arguments.
  • Another compelling argument put forward is …
  • This convenience, as critics mention, makes …
  • For all these reasons, many people …

Accepting, then disagreeing

  • As plausible as these arguments are, I side with …
  • While I acknowledge …, I believe …
  • It is true that …; however, …

Adding and explaining

  • For one thing, …
  • In addition, …
  • In light of this, …
  • …, all of which reduce …
  • … such as …, …, and …

Concluding

  • Ultimately, while I acknowledge …, I am firmly of the view that …
  • In conclusion, …
  • To sum up, …

Common mistakes

Task

  • Writing only one view.
  • Giving no opinion, or hiding it until the last line.
  • Changing your opinion between paragraphs.

Ideas

  • Many ideas with no explanation.
  • New ideas in the conclusion.
  • Examples that are too personal (“my uncle…”).

Language

  • Contractions: don’t, can’t, it’s.
  • Copying the question word for word.
  • Repeating “I think” in every paragraph.

Layout

  • One long paragraph.
  • A very long introduction.
  • Only “firstly, secondly, thirdly” as linking.
